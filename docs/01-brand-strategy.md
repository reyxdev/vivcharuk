## 1.1 The strategic problem

Вівчарик is a real factory competing in a category dominated by two failure modes:

1. **The souvenir stall.** Ukrainian wool goods are overwhelmingly sold through marketplace
   listings and Instagram DMs. Photography is flat, copy is a specification dump, and there
   is no evidence of manufacturing. Price is the only differentiator, so price falls.
2. **The folk pastiche.** Sites that reach for authenticity by layering embroidery borders,
   wooden textures, and script fonts. This signals "tourist trinket", not "manufacturer", and
   it caps the achievable price point.

Both failures share one root cause: **they show the product but not the production.** A wool
blanket photographed on a white background is a commodity. The same blanket photographed
leaving a carding machine in a named workshop in Яворів is a provenance story, and provenance
is what supports premium pricing.

### The strategic bet

> Вівчарик does not sell wool products. It sells **verified origin**.

Every design and engineering decision in this blueprint is downstream of that sentence. The
factory is the product's most valuable asset and it is currently invisible. Making it visible
— at scale, throughout the site, not confined to an "About us" page — is the entire strategy.

## 1.2 Positioning statement

> For people who want textiles that will outlast them, **Вівчарик** is a **Carpathian wool
> factory** that has run the full production cycle under one roof for
> {{FOUNDING_YEAR}}-to-today, because a blanket is only as honest as the hands and machines that
> made it, and we can show you every one of them — **in Яворів**, the village the Hutsul region
> calls its lizhnyk capital.

The sentence is deliberately built in two moves, and the order is the strategy.
[00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F6 rejected the proposal to promote Яворів
into the approved tagline, and the reasoning holds for the positioning statement too: **«Карпати»
is the term of recognition and «Яворів» is the term of proof.** The first clause has to be
understood by a reader who has never heard of the village — including every foreign buyer — and
the second clause is what that reader is given once they are paying attention. Leading with Яворів
inverts that and asks the reader to learn a proper noun before they have any reason to care.

§1.2b sets out the Yavoriv argument in full, because the second clause is the one that does the
commercial work even though it is not the one that goes in the headline.

### Positioning map

Two axes that matter in this category: *evidence of manufacturing* (low → high) and *design
register* (folk → modern).

```
                          MODERN
                             │
                             │        ◆ ВІВЧАРИК
                             │         (target)
                             │
  low evidence ──────────────┼────────────────── high evidence
                             │
        ○ marketplace        │       ○ small workshops with
          resellers          │         good product, no craft in
                             │         their digital presence
                          FOLK
```

The upper-right quadrant is empty in this category in Ukraine. That is the position to take,
and it is defensible: competitors cannot copy it without owning a factory.

## 1.2b The Yavoriv claim — the substantiating layer, not the headline

[00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E2 resolved the address to **вул. Петруші,
с. Яворів, Косівський район, Івано-Франківська область, 78644**. It is not Вербовець; that was
the adjacent business, roughly 20 km away, and the separation is useful in its own right because
it keeps the two NAP records from colliding.

The address is not an operations detail. It is the most valuable brand asset on the project, and
it arrived after the rest of this strategy was written.

### The governing pattern: «Карпати» to be understood, «Яворів» to be believed

[00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F6 settles where each word goes, and the
ruling is worth stating as a principle rather than as a copy edit, because it recurs on every page
of this blueprint:

> **The headline earns attention. The pages beneath it earn trust. Those are different jobs, and
> they take different words.**

«Карпати» is understood instantly by every audience this site serves, including the `en`, `pl` and
`de` buyers who have never seen a Ukrainian map. «Яворів» requires knowledge the reader may not
have at the moment a headline reaches them — and **a tagline is not the place to teach a new proper
noun.** A reader who has to stop and work out what a word means has stopped reading the sentence.

That constraint disappears the moment the reader is inside a page. By the time they are reading
§21.6, watching a stage video on `/vyrobnytstvo`, or scanning a PDP origin block, they have already
decided to spend attention — and specificity is exactly what that attention was spent looking for.
So the Yavoriv material is not diluted; it is **relocated to where it converts**.

| Surface | Word | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| Tagline, homepage hero H1, about-page hero H1 | **«в Карпатах»** — the approved D1 copy, unchanged | Recognition. The reader has committed nothing yet |
| Hero overlines, section overlines, breadcrumb context | **«с. Яворів, Косівський район»** | Adjacent to the headline but subordinate to it — context, not the claim itself |
| Production page, about page, contact page | **Яворів named and explained in full** — the lizhnyk capital, the museum, the plein airs, the carving dynasties ([20-production-page-specification.md](20-production-page-specification.md), [21-about-page-specification.md](21-about-page-specification.md)) | The reader is here voluntarily and is looking for exactly this |
| PDP origin block, `Place` and `ImageObject` structured data, NAP | **Яворів, in full** | Machine-readable proof and the buyer's verification step |
| Meta titles and descriptions | Яворів where it serves a specific query; «Карпати» in general titles | Query intent decides, not house style |
| Product names | Optional. «Ліжник Яворівський» stays available, not imposed | A strong asset where it fits; not a mandate |
| Journal articles | Яворів as a subject in its own right | Editorial has room to teach a proper noun; a headline does not |

Everything below in this section is therefore the argument for the *second* layer. It is the
stronger argument commercially, and it is still not the one that goes in the headline.

### Why «яворівський» is the specificity claim that «карпатський» cannot be

Яворів is the recognised centre of Hutsul lizhnyk weaving — commonly «столиця ліжникарства». It
holds a dedicated Музей ліжникарства, it hosts annual lizhnyk-weaving plein airs attended by art
historians from Kyiv, Lviv and Ivano-Frankivsk, and it is the home village of the **Шкрібляк** and
**Корпанюк** woodcarving dynasties.

| Claim | Who else can make it | What it does for price |
|---|---|---|
| «Карпатська вовна» | Thousands of sellers, including importers who have never been to the Carpathians | Nothing. It is the category's baseline noise and the buyer has learned to discount it |
| «Ліжник з Яворова» | Only producers actually in Яворів, and the claim is checkable on a map in four seconds | Functions as an **appellation** — the same mechanism by which Champagne, Parmigiano and Harris Tweed convert geography into price |

This is not a stylistic preference. §1.4 "Rooted" already demands specificity of place over
generic national symbolism; Яворів is the specific place, and until E2 the document did not know
its name. The word therefore belongs on the production and about pages, in the overlines, in the
meta titles, in the `Place` structured data, and in the product names themselves («Ліжник
Яворівський» is a stronger asset than any generic name it replaces —
[00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E5). It does **not** belong in the tagline
([00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F6), and the reason is not that the argument
above is wrong — it is that a headline is read by someone who has not yet agreed to learn anything.

**Substitution rule for copy.** Use «яворівський» wherever the sentence is making a *provenance*
argument **inside a page**. Use «карпатський» where the sentence is describing a *region* to a
reader who has not yet been told about Яворів — every headline, and principally the `en`, `pl` and
`de` locales, where the correct construction is "from Yavoriv in the Ukrainian Carpathians": the
specific name first, the recognisable region as its gloss. Dropping the region entirely abroad
trades a real advantage for an unparseable one, which is the same error F6 prevented in `uk`.

### The verification constraint, which is absolute

Hutsul lizhnyk weaving is widely described as inscribed on Ukraine's national register of
intangible cultural heritage. **The exact status and the exact wording must be confirmed before
any sentence referencing it is published** ([00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md)
E2, open item 4).

Two rules bind regardless of what the confirmation says:

1. **The craft may be listed. A company is not.** No copy, badge, footer line or structured-data
   property may imply that Вівчарик itself holds a heritage designation. «Ліжникарство внесено
   до…» is a statement about the craft; «Вівчарик — об'єкт культурної спадщини» is false.
2. **It is not a certificate.** §1.5 forbids certificate imagery and §21.12 lists it as a deny-list
   claim. A heritage reference is editorial prose in a sentence, never a seal.

Unconfirmed, the claim is simply omitted. The Yavoriv argument above is already strong enough
without it, which is precisely why there is no reason to rush it.

### The third-order effect: link building

The museum, the plein airs and the craft associations are locally relevant referrers of exactly
the kind a cold-start domain cannot otherwise earn
([02-ux-research.md](02-ux-research.md) §2.1). A domain with no authority and no social presence
has very few routes to a first inbound link; being physically located in the village those
institutions are about is one of them.

## 1.3 Brand architecture

Single master brand. No sub-brands at launch. Product families are described in the master
brand's voice.

One structural exception is reserved: **Вівчарик Ательє** (atelier) as the eventual home for
one-of-one handmade lizhnyks and the future wooden products. This is documented now so the
design system can accommodate a second tier later — a dedicated accent treatment and a
distinct PDP layout — without a redesign. It is not built at launch (see
[00-assumptions.md](00-assumptions.md) B1).

### The wooden category is no longer a bolt-on

The future **ДЕРЕВО** category ([00-client-decisions.md](00-client-decisions.md) D3) read, before
E2, as an unrelated line that a wool brand would struggle to justify — the classic gift-shop
drift that dilutes a manufacturing claim. The location resolves that retroactively: Яворів is the
home village of the Шкрібляк and Корпанюк woodcarving dynasties, so woodcarving is **the second
craft of the same village**, not a second business.

That changes how the category must launch when it launches. Its story is *place*, not *product
range*: the same village, the other craft. It is also the one category where partner sourcing is
strategically comfortable rather than awkward, because a curated selection of Yavoriv carving is
a coherent proposition in a way that a curated selection of generic housewares is not — subject
in full to the labelling rule in §1.7b.

No build work at launch. Recorded here so that the category is designed from the provenance story
rather than retro-fitted with one.

## 1.4 Brand personality

Five traits, each with an explicit behavioural consequence for the build. A trait that does
not change a build decision is decoration, so every trait below carries one.

| Trait | Means | Does *not* mean | Build consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grounded** | Plain statements of fact. Numbers, dates, materials, weights. | Boastful superlatives. | Copy is declarative. No "best", "unique", "amazing". Specifications are surfaced early on the PDP, not buried in a tab. |
| **Warm** | Human presence. Named people, visible hands, workshop sound. | Sentimental or folksy. | Every production photo must contain a person or evidence of one. Stock-style empty-machine shots are rejected at art direction. **The named people now exist** — see below. |
| **Patient** | Slow processes shown at their real pace. | Slow interface. | Motion is unhurried (500–900 ms for narrative reveals) but interaction feedback is immediate (<100 ms). These are different clocks. See [13-motion-system.md](13-motion-system.md). |
| **Exacting** | Craft precision mirrored in interface precision. | Cold or clinical. | Optical alignment, consistent spacing rhythm, no visual bugs. A misaligned grid undermines a craftsmanship claim more than customers can articulate. |
| **Rooted** | Specific to **Яворів** everywhere the reader has context; «Карпати» in the headline that gets them there. | Generically "Ukrainian", or «Карпати» used as the *only* geographic descriptor anywhere below a headline. | The village name, the district, the street and the road appear in body copy, overlines, structured data and NAP. «Карпати» alone is not a provenance statement, but it is the correct *headline* statement (§1.2b, [00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F6). |

### "Warm" is now buildable — the named people

[00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E1 resolves the people, which removes the
largest gap in this trait. A brand personality that demands named humans and has none is a mood
board; these are the humans.

| Person | Role | Where the name appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Гондурак Іван Федорович** | Owner of production | About-page narrative, production-page people section, machinery and process attribution |
| **Гондурак Любов Юріївна** | Deputy owner of production; **seller of record (ФОП)** | About-page narrative, production-page people section, and separately in the legal and transactional surfaces |

Three rules follow:

1. **Written consent is still required for photographs and verbatim quotes**, ownership
   notwithstanding ([20-production-page-specification.md](20-production-page-specification.md)
   §20.10). Being the owner is not consent to be published across four locales, and GDPR applies
   to the `de` and `pl` audiences.
2. **The editorial use and the legal use are different registers and must not be merged.**
   «Іван і Любов Гондураки» is the about page. `ФОП Гондурак Любов Юріївна` is the offer contract,
   the invoice, the Impressum and the checkout footer. A narrative page that reads like a
   registration record loses the warmth; a legal page that reads like a narrative is defective.
3. **Two named people is a positioning fact, not a limitation.** Persona 3's killer objection is
   "is this a factory or a reseller?" ([02-ux-research.md](02-ux-research.md) §2.3) — a reseller
   does not put the owner's name and mobile number on the page.

## 1.5 Verbal identity

### Voice principles

1. **Show the work, don't claim the quality.** "Carded twice, spun in-house" beats "premium
   quality". Concrete process descriptions are self-authenticating; adjectives are not.
2. **Numbers over adverbs.** "1,400 g of Carpathian wool" not "beautifully heavy".
3. **Name things.** The machine, **the village — Яворів**, the person — **Іван**, **Любов** —
   and the year. Named entities are also what AI search engines extract and cite: the voice
   principle and the GEO strategy are the same principle. See
   [30-ai-search-optimization.md](30-ai-search-optimization.md). The one entity that must **not**
   be named is a partner manufacturer (§1.7b), and that exception is a client instruction, not a
   preference.
4. **Short sentences for facts. Longer sentences for story.** Rhythm distinguishes the two
   registers without needing labels.
5. **Never apologise for the price.** State what produces it.

### The tagline — decided, and no longer open

[00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F6 closes this. The client-approved D1 copy
stands **unchanged**:

> **«Понад 30 років виробляємо натуральні вовняні вироби в Карпатах.»**

The proposal to substitute «у Яворові» is **withdrawn**, and the withdrawal is correct for the
reason given in §1.2b: the tagline's job is recognition, not proof. Nothing further is to be
tested, proposed or A/B'd on this sentence — `uk` is settled. What remains open is only its
translation into `en`, `pl` and `de`, where "in the Carpathians" carries the same recognition value
and needs no adaptation beyond idiom.

**Supporting lines, which are a different problem and remain a client decision.** A tagline is one
sentence on every surface; these are the lines that sit beneath it on individual surfaces, where
the reader has already arrived and specificity is an asset rather than a tax:

| Line | Register | Where it belongs |
|---|---|---|
| **«Ліжники з Яворова — села, яке називають столицею ліжникарства»** ([00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E2) | The appellation, stated in full | Homepage hero **subline** beneath the approved tagline, about-page H1 support, production-page intro. Never the headline itself |
| **«Вовна, яку ми знаємо на дотик»** | Craft-forward, warm, no folklore; translates cleanly to all four locales | The compact brand-surface lockup — header, footer, packaging — where even the approved tagline is too long |
| «Повний цикл. Одні руки.» | Manufacturing-forward, and **literally true** since E6 confirmed the full cycle end to end including hides | Wholesale page headline ([19-wholesale-page-specification.md](19-wholesale-page-specification.md)) |
| «З гори до вашого дому» | Most accessible, weakest differentiation | Not recommended. It is the one line that carries neither the manufacturing claim nor the place |

The division of labour is the same one F6 drew: the approved tagline gets the reader to stop, and
the subline is where Яворів does its work.

### Words the brand does not use

`ексклюзивний`, `елітний`, `неперевершений`, `100% натуральний` without a certificate to back
it, `еко` as a standalone claim, and any use of `автентичний` in a headline — authenticity is
demonstrated, never asserted. The moment a brand says "authentic", it is not.

## 1.6 Visual identity thesis

The visual system rests on three material references drawn from the factory itself, not from
a mood board:

1. **Raw fleece** — soft, irregular, off-white, light-scattering. Becomes: the background
   family (Wool White, Warm Cream), grain overlays, blur-based depth.
2. **Dyed yarn and forest** — dense, saturated, absorbing. Becomes: the Forest/Emerald
   greens used for type, surfaces, and the dark mode inversion.
3. **Machine and mountain** — hard edges, steel, stone, geometry. Becomes: the grid, the
   rule lines, the restraint. This reference exists specifically to prevent the design from
   becoming soft and pillowy, which is the failure mode of a wool brand.

### Carpathian ornament — the rule

Traditional Hutsul ornament is used as **structure, not decoration**. Concretely:

- Permitted: derived geometry driving section dividers, a single-motif loading mark, the
  repeat rhythm of a background pattern at ≤4% opacity, and the shape language of custom
  icons.
- Forbidden: ornament as a border around content, ornament over photography, ornament in more
  than one accent colour per viewport, ornament at decorative scale on a product page.

Test: if removing the ornament makes the layout *fall apart*, it is structural and allowed. If
removing it makes the layout *plainer*, it is decoration and must be removed.

Ornament is sourced from the {{ORNAMENT_SOURCE}} tradition and must be reviewed by the client
for regional correctness. Using the wrong region's motif is a credibility error that the
target audience — particularly Ukrainian buyers and diaspora — will detect immediately.

**E2 narrows the source and removes most of that risk.** Яворів is the home village of the
Шкрібляк and Корпанюк carving dynasties, so the regionally correct ornament is the **Hutsul
Kosiv-district geometric carving tradition of the village itself** — the same visual language the
future ДЕРЕВО category will sell (§1.3). `{{ORNAMENT_SOURCE}}` should resolve to that rather than
to a generic Ukrainian sampler, and the resolution should be confirmed against the Музей
ліжникарства's own material rather than against a stock-vector pack. Two consequences:

- The ornament and the product line share one origin, which means the design system is not
  borrowing a decorative language — it is rendering the village's own.
- Specific named dynasties are checkable. That is the same property §1.8 ranks evidence by, and
  it is why this is worth resolving properly rather than approximating.

## 1.7 The mascot — confirmed brand core

**Resolved by the client** ([00-client-decisions.md](00-client-decisions.md) D2): the
sheep/shepherd identity is mandated and the visual brand is built around it. This is no longer
a decision to weigh.

It is also the right call, for a reason worth stating: **Вівчарик means *little shepherd*.**
The mascot is not an ornament attached to a luxury brand — it is the brand's name made visible.
A wool manufacturer named after a shepherd that then refuses to draw one would be hiding from
its own story.

**The tension that remains:** a *cartoon* mascot would still pull against the premium
positioning in §1.2. The brands cited as references — Aesop, Hermès, Arc'teryx — have no
mascots, and that is not a coincidence. Mascots signal approachability, which trades against
perceived price.

**The resolution:** the mascot is built as a *drawn mark*, not a cartoon character. This
execution discipline is what allows a shepherd to sit next to a 14,900 UAH price point.

| Dimension | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Style | Single-weight line drawing, ink-on-paper register, no fill, no eyes-with-highlights | Reads as a woodcut or a maker's mark, not a game asset |
| Colour | Forest Green on light, Wool White on dark. Never more than one colour. | Prevents drift toward cartoon |
| Presence | Loader, empty states, 404, order confirmation, footer mark, seasonal easter egg | High-delight, low-stakes moments only |
| Absence | **Never on the PDP, cart, checkout, or wholesale page** | These are the trust-critical and money-critical surfaces |
| Cursor tracking | Head rotation only, max ±12°, damped, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion` and on touch | Subtle enough to be noticed on the second visit, not the first |
| Idle sleep | After 60 s of no input | Rewards the curious without demanding attention |

This gives the brand its shepherd and the positioning its restraint. Full motion specification
in [34-animation-storyboard.md](34-animation-storyboard.md).

## 1.7b The partner-goods problem

The client confirms a category of **products from other manufacturers**
([00-client-decisions.md](00-client-decisions.md) D3). This is the single largest threat to the
strategy in §1.2, and it has to be addressed here rather than deferred to the catalogue spec.

**The threat, stated plainly.** The entire positioning rests on *verified origin*. A resold
product sitting undifferentiated beside an own-made one converts that promise into a lie the
moment a customer notices — and wholesale buyers, the most commercially valuable audience, are
the ones who will check.

**The reframe.** A manufacturer that also *curates* is not weaker than one that only sells
itself; it is an authority rather than merely a supplier. A customer who trusts Вівчарик to
judge wool will accept Вівчарик's judgement about someone else's wool. That trust only
transfers when the distinction is **volunteered**, not discovered.

So the rule is the inverse of the instinct:

> **Label partner goods more loudly than you think is comfortable.**
> Hidden, it is a deception found later. Stated, it is a curation credential.

### What Round 2 changed, stated honestly

[00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E7 asked whether partner manufacturers may
be named. The answer was **«Ні.»**

That weakens the reframe above, and this document should say so rather than assert that the
fallback is equivalent. It is not.

| | Naming the partner (unavailable) | The fallback (what ships) |
|---|---|---|
| What the buyer learns | A specific maker they can look up | That the item is not own-made, and roughly where it is from |
| Mechanism | Verifiable. The claim is falsifiable, which is what makes it evidence (§1.8) | Unverifiable. The buyer is asked to take the disclosure on trust |
| Strategic effect | A genuine curation credential — Вівчарик's judgement attached to a named third party | An honest disclosure. Not a credential |
| Where it sits in §1.8's evidence ranking | Rank 3–4 territory | Below rank 6 — it is policy clarity, not evidence |

Calling the fallback a curation credential would be the same move this document criticises
elsewhere: asserting a quality instead of showing it. The accurate description is narrower —
**it is an honest disclosure that costs some conversion and buys the right not to be caught.**
That is still worth paying for, and it is the correct trade, but it should be priced correctly in
the plan rather than talked up.

### The rule the constraint does not relax

Being unable to name the partner is a reason to be **more** explicit that an item is not own-made,
not less. The temptation runs the other way — a label with no name in it looks like an unfinished
label, and the instinct will be to shrink it. Resist that. An unnamed partner disclosure that is
also visually quiet is functionally a concealment, and it fails the one test this section exists
to pass.

**The labelling specification, per E7:**

| Case | Public label |
|---|---|
| Own manufacture | «Власне виробництво» |
| Partner, region known | «Відібрано Вівчариком» + «Виготовлено карпатським майстром» (`partnerRegion`: «Косівщина», «Гуцульщина») |
| Partner, region unknown | «Відібрано Вівчариком» + «Виготовлено іншим виробником» |

`Product.partnerName` **stays null and is never rendered** — not in a tooltip, not in an accordion,
not in structured data, not in an `alt` attribute. A field that is populated but hidden leaks.

Unchanged and binding: the partner mark sits at **equal visual weight** to «Власне виробництво»,
the origin facet stays **pinned at the top of the filter panel**, and homepage, hero, production
storytelling and best-seller rails show **own manufacture only** — the brand surfaces make the
manufacturing claim and the catalogue holds the breadth. `manufacturer` is **omitted** from the
JSON-LD of partner goods rather than set to Вівчарик; omission is honest, misattribution is not.

### What Round 3 changed: partner goods carry the Вівчарик brand

[00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F3 answers the question E7 left open.
Partner-manufactured items are **sold under the Вівчарик brand**.

The structured-data consequence is exact, and it is the distinction schema.org was built to draw:

| Origin | `brand` | `manufacturer` |
|---|---|---|
| `OWN_MANUFACTURE` | `Вівчарик` | `Вівчарик` |
| `PARTNER_MANUFACTURE` | `Вівчарик` | **Omitted entirely.** Never set to Вівчарик, never set to a placeholder, never set to the unnamed partner |

`brand` states the name the item is sold under, which is true. `manufacturer` states who made it,
which is a fact this project is not permitted to publish — so it is omitted rather than filled in.
Omission asserts nothing false; substitution would assert something false in a machine-readable
field, which is the worst place to do it.

**The tension this creates, stated plainly rather than smoothed over.** Selling another workshop's
goods under your own brand is ordinary retail practice and entirely legitimate. It nonetheless sits
against the thesis in §1.2 — *the brand sells verified origin* — because **the brand name now
appears on items the brand did not make.** A document that claimed this was unproblematic would be
doing exactly what §1.7b criticises elsewhere: asserting a quality instead of examining it.

**It does not change the plan. It raises the stakes on the on-page label.** Every mitigation above
stays exactly as specified, and each one is now load-bearing rather than precautionary:

- «Відібрано Вівчариком» keeps **equal visual weight** to «Власне виробництво». The branding makes
  the temptation to shrink it stronger, which is the reason to write the rule down again.
- The origin facet stays **pinned first** in the filter panel.
- Partner goods stay **excluded** from the homepage, the hero, the production storytelling and the
  best-seller rail.

The asymmetry that governs the whole section is unchanged and now carries more weight: **a customer
who discovers the distinction themselves feels misled; a customer who was told plainly feels
informed.** The label is the entire difference between those two outcomes, and putting the brand
name on the goods makes it matter more, not less.

Implementation in [00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E7,
[00-client-decisions.md](00-client-decisions.md) §D3 and
[25-database-schema.md](25-database-schema.md). Comprehension testing is
[02-ux-research.md](02-ux-research.md) §2.8 R5, which E7 makes more urgent rather than less: the
wording being tested is now the weaker wording.

## 1.8 Trust architecture

Trust must be established in the first 3 seconds, and the mechanism for that is **evidence,
not badges**. Badges are the weakest available trust signal because anyone can render one.
Ranked by strength:

| Rank | Signal | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Video of the actual factory in motion | Homepage hero, production page |
| 2 | Named, faced people doing the work | Homepage §2, about, production |
| 3 | Photographed process with dates and locations | Production page, blog, PDP origin block |
| 4 | Specific verifiable numbers (weight, density, fibre length, year) | PDP spec table, stat strips |
| 5 | Customer reviews with photos | PDP, homepage §8 |
| 6 | Policy clarity — returns, shipping, warranty stated without hedging | Footer, PDP trust row, checkout |
| 7 | Badges and icons | PDP trust row, footer — supporting role only |

The design must therefore be **photography-led**, which is why photography availability is a
BLOCKER assumption ([00-assumptions.md](00-assumptions.md) E1) rather than a nice-to-have.
There is no version of this strategy that survives placeholder imagery.

### The signal that outranks the table: a shop attached to the production floor

[00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F2 confirms that the Яворів address houses
**both retail and production** — «там знаходиться і магазин і виробництво». This is new, and it
sits above rank 1.

Every signal in the table above is something the brand *shows* the visitor. A shop is something the
visitor can *go to*. A factory you can only read about is a claim, however well filmed; a shop you
can walk into, attached to the production floor, in a village tourists already travel to for this
exact craft, is the claim already settled before the site makes it. No on-page element outperforms
an address the reader could drive to this afternoon, and none of the category's resellers can
produce one.

Three consequences that belong to strategy rather than to the contact page:

1. **It is the answer to the category's primary purchase anxiety.** "Is this a real factory or a
   reseller" ([02-ux-research.md](02-ux-research.md) §2.4 A3) is answered more completely by a
   visitable shop than by anything this document can specify.
2. **It is a cold-start channel that does not depend on search.** With no social presence
   ([00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E3) and no domain authority, physical
   footfall in a craft-tourism village is one of the few launch channels available, and it is the
   only one that costs nothing.
3. **It converts the appellation in §1.2b from an argument into an invitation.** «Ліжники з
   Яворова» is a stronger sentence when the next line is «приїздіть подивитися».

The invitation is specified on the surfaces that carry it:
[20-production-page-specification.md](20-production-page-specification.md) §20.11 and
[21-about-page-specification.md](21-about-page-specification.md) §21.10, with the variable-hours
posture from [00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E3 — the hours are never
published, and the call-ahead line is framed as courtesy rather than caveat.

### What Round 2 solved, and what it explicitly did not

[00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E5 permits reusing the adjacent business's
products and photographs. That is a large de-risking — but only of *catalogue coverage*, and the
distinction matters because it is easy to read the permission as closing E1 entirely.

| Requirement | Status after E5 |
|---|---|
| A product image for every SKU | **Solved** by reuse, subject to re-crop, re-grade, EXIF strip, semantic filenames and newly authored `alt` |
| Rank 1–3 evidence — the factory, the process, the machines, the people, **in Яворів** | **Not solved.** Every reusable frame documents a different workshop in a different village |

The strategy in §1.1 is "show the production". Reused photographs show *a* production. Publishing
them under a Yavoriv provenance claim would make this document's central argument false in its
first execution, which is a worse outcome than having fewer pictures.

**So the shoot is descoped, not cancelled.** It shrinks from a full catalogue production to **one
or two days in Яворів** covering factory, process, machinery, people and place. That is a far
cheaper dependency than the one E1 originally described, and it remains a launch blocker for the
homepage hero, the production page and the about page.

### The self-policing constraint on manufacturing claims

[00-client-decisions-2.md](00-client-decisions-2.md) E6 confirms the full cycle from raw material
to finished goods, hides included. A stronger claim raises the evidence burden in the same motion,
so one rule binds every manufacturing surface:

> **Photograph the stages you claim. A stage that cannot be photographed is not asserted.**

This is deliberately self-enforcing. It needs no auditor, it converts a verbal claim into a
production task with a visible output, and it means the page can never drift further than the
camera went. It also folds cleanly into the Yavoriv shoot above rather than adding a new
dependency. Applied in
[20-production-page-specification.md](20-production-page-specification.md) §20.2.

The «бельгійська технологія» framing is **removed from the brand vocabulary entirely**. It
belonged to the adjacent business, it was never Вівчарик's claim to make, and inheriting a
borrowed technique name would be the precise failure §1.7b exists to prevent.

## 1.9 Competitive differentiation summary

| Axis | Category norm | Вівчарик |
|---|---|---|
| Product presentation | Cut-out on white | In-situ, in-process, in-place |
| Origin claim | "Made in Ukraine", or at best "Карпати" — and that is the whole claim | «Карпати» in the headline, then **Яворів** beneath it — named village, named owners (Іван and Любов Гондурак), filmed process. Two layers where the category has one (§1.2b) |
| Provenance mechanism | A word in the title anyone can type | An appellation the buyer can check on a map (§1.2b) |
| Verifiability | None available. There is nowhere to go | **A shop attached to the production floor**, at the named address, in the village the craft is named after ([00-client-decisions-3.md](00-client-decisions-3.md) F2) |
| Price justification | Discount from a fake RRP | Material specification and labour hours |
| Wholesale | A phone number | A qualified landing page with capacity, MOQ, and private-label offer |
| Content | None, or copied specs | Editorial magazine on wool, care, and Carpathian craft |
| Technology | Marketplace or template | Custom, fast, accessible, structured for AI retrieval |
